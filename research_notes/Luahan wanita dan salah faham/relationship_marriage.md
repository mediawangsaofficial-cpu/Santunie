## Which specific viral posts (with URL and date) show this misconception?

### Takeaway
I found 9 usable cases. About 5 show a misconception held by the woman herself: self-blame for divorce, "his porn use means I'm not good enough", exposing a husband's aib online, a wife arranging poligami herself, and "kerja rumah 100% tugas suami" presented as settled. The other cases are "reverse" ones, where the woman is broadly right and the husband or netizens hold the misconception (nafkah). These reverse cases are still useful for showing the *corrected* understanding.

### Cited Findings

**Case 1: "Saya selekeh, tak pandai layan suami", wife blames herself for the divorce** (date unclear; search summary says about two years old, so roughly 2023–2024; OLDER CASE)
- (a) A woman told a third party that she was divorced because she was untidy ("selekeh") and "didn't know how to serve her husband". She had ordered lingerie to win him back, but was divorced before she could "fix her shortcomings", and later asked for the lingerie to be given away. The story came via an X/Twitter user (@daliajepp) writing about one of her clients. Netizens were angered ("naik angin"), mostly at the husband. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/saya-selekeh-tak-pandai-layan-suami-punca-wanita-ini-bercerai-buat-netizen-naik-angin); [Kisah Dunia](https://kisahdunia.com/order-lingerie-niat-nak-tackle-balik-suami-wanita-pasrah-ditalak-sebab-selekeh/)
- (b) Misconception: "If my husband leaves me, it is entirely my fault for not being attractive or serving him enough." In other words, the wife alone carries the duty to keep the marriage going.
- (c) Correction: In Islamic jurisprudence as presented by the Federal Territory Mufti, a *husband* can also be nusyuz (failing his marital duties). Marital obligations run both ways. — [Muftiwp Irsyad al-Fatwa 371: Nusyuz bagi seorang suami](https://muftiwp.gov.my/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum/4005-irsyad-al-fatwa-siri-ke-371-nusyuz-bagi-seorang-suami)
- Nuance or contest: Taking care of one's appearance for one's spouse is encouraged in Islamic teaching, so her wish to improve is not "wrong". The error is the *total self-blame*. The story is second-hand (relayed by a third party), so it is unverified.

**Case 2: Wife feels "not good enough" because of her husband's alleged porn addiction** (MustShareNews, Threads post on "Monday, 12 Jan". The year is unclear: the search summary noted inconsistent metadata, about 303 days before Nov 2026, which suggests January 2026, but this is unconfirmed)
- (a) A Malaysian woman asked netizens on Threads for advice after finding that her husband kept consuming pornography. She said it made her feel inadequate and "not good enough" as his wife. Many netizens told her to divorce him, saying she seemed unable to change his behaviour despite repeated efforts. — [MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/husband-loves-pornography/)
- (b) Misconceptions: (i) the woman's belief that her husband's porn use reflects *her* inadequacy; (ii) the netizens' reflex that "red flag = cerai segera".
- (c) Correction: I could not find a Malaysian psychologist or counsellor source specifically on this point (see Gaps). On the Islamic side, the Perlis Mufti's view on aib (Case 3) is relevant if she were to name him publicly. Her Threads post sought advice anonymously, which falls closer to the "seeking advice" exception (see Case 3).

**Case 3: Wives exposing a husband's cheating or aib on social media** (Kosmo article with the Perlis Mufti's view, date unclear; related Kosmo case dated 29 Sept 2025)
- (a) Viral posts and videos by wives exposing cheating husbands are a recurring pattern. In a Sept 2025 case, a cheating man was caught at a health clinic. His wife then *asked the public to stop spreading* the viral video of her family's private matter. — [Kosmo, 29 Sept 2025](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2025/09/29/isteri-dicurangi-mohon-henti-sebar-video-di-klinik/)
- (b) Misconception: "Saya mangsa, jadi saya boleh dedahkan aib suami di media sosial."
- (c) Correction: Mufti Perlis Dr. Mohd Asri Zainul Abidin, answering on the Perlis Mufti Department website as reported by Kosmo, said:
  - publicly exposing a husband's private faults is haram and a major sin (dosa besar);
  - "satu dosa tidak menghalalkan dosa yang lain";
  - turning netizens into a "mahkamah" that judges private sins is haram;
  - the lawful routes are to object directly to the husband or take the case to the Syariah court. — [Kosmo](https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=761173)
- Other views in the same direction:
  - The Penang Mufti said there is no need to disclose such matters publicly, and that a wronged wife who exposes her husband is still sinful.
  - A Negeri Sembilan Deputy Mufti said sharing such matters online is sinful. — [search summary of Kosmo/Utusan coverage](https://www.utusan.com.my/pancaindera/jejak-ulama/2023/10/berdosa-sebar-aib-pasangan-individu-di-media-sosial/); [Utusan 2023](https://www.utusan.com.my/pancaindera/jejak-ulama/2023/05/jangan-sebarkan-aib-suami-isteri-di-media-sosial/)
- Nuance (contested edges): Scholars allow revealing someone's faults in limited cases, such as complaining of injustice (zulm), seeking legal advice, or seeking a solution from an authority. This is *not* for gaining public sympathy or making viral content. — [SAYS: Hukum dedah konflik rumah tangga di media sosial](https://says.com/my/gaya-hidup/hukum-dedah-konflik-rumah-tangga-di-media-sosial-tak-semua-perkara-layak-jadi-content). Where an anonymous "minta nasihat" post ends and an aib-exposing post begins is a grey area.
- Related ruling: the Pahang Mufti has a ruling on wives doing TikTok Live. — [Mufti Pahang PDF](https://mufti.pahang.gov.my/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/Hukum-Isteri-buat-live-tiktok.pdf) (content not read; cite only as existing)

**Case 4: Wife publicly searching on Threads for a second wife ("calon madu") for her husband** (Kosmo, 25 Jan 2026)
- (a) A woman posted on Threads looking for a candidate second wife for her husband, which drew mixed reactions from Threads users. — [Kosmo, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2026/01/25/isteri-cari-calon-bini-kedua-suami/)
- Similar earlier cases:
  - A wife who "doesn't mind" her husband remarrying and even does the risik for the future madu, calling herself an "insan terpilih" (date unclear). — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/video-insan-terpilih-isteri-tak-kisah-suami-kahwin-lagi-siap-risik-untuk-bakal-madu)
  - A wife who allowed a second marriage on condition that the madu has no children. — [Kosmo, 11 Dec 2023](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2023/12/11/isteri-pertama-izin-kahwin-dua-syaratnya-madu-tak-boleh-ada-anak/) (OLDER CASE)
  - A wife who proposed to her neighbour on her husband's behalf. — [TheRakyatPost, 24 Aug 2023](https://www.therakyatpost.com/trpbm/2023/08/24/10-tahun-berumah-tangga-wanita-sanggup-berpoligami-siap-lamar-jiran-untuk-suami/) (OLDER CASE)
- (b) Misconceptions: (i) "the wife's izin or arrangement is what makes poligami OK or valid"; (ii) a wife can lawfully impose conditions such as "madu tak boleh ada anak".
- (c) Correction (legal):
  - Under Malaysian Islamic family law, a polygamous marriage requires the **prior written permission of the Syariah Court**. The existing wife's consent is not legally what is required, though the court considers her views under s.23(3) of the Islamic Family Law (Federal Territories) Act 1984 [Act 303]. The court examines the husband's assets, income, dependants and commitments. — [UIS e-prosiding PASAK5](https://conference.uis.edu.my/pasak5/images/eprosidingpasak5/ID045.pdf); [Act 303 text, LawPlayer](https://lawplayer.com/my/act/act-303)
  - s.23(4) sets conditions meant to protect the wives' rights. — [same UIS paper](https://conference.uis.edu.my/pasak5/images/eprosidingpasak5/ID045.pdf)
- Contested: Whether a wife *should* facilitate poligami is a matter of personal choice and religious view, not a "misconception" as such. The clear factual error is about legal procedure. I found no source on whether a "no children" condition is enforceable (see Gaps).

**Case 5: "Kerja rumah tugas suami, bukan isteri", a wife's viral post after the tok kadi's question** (SAYS, date unclear)
- (a) A woman's post went viral after a tok kadi asked during the marriage ceremony who should do the cooking and laundry. The post (and the husband's response as summarised) said housework is really the husband's responsibility. Husbands may ask, but cannot be angry if the wife refuses; wives usually do it because they were raised to see it as their job. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/masak-cuci-kain-tugas-siapa-isteri-terkejut-dengar-jawapan-suami-bila-tok-kadi-tanya)
- (b) Possible misconceptions, which are *contested*:
  - the view "isteri tak wajib buat kerja rumah, so I owe nothing" when presented as settled consensus;
  - conversely, netizens and in-laws who treat it as an absolute duty of the wife. Kosmo reported netizens criticising a mother-in-law's "old view" that a wife must meet all her husband's needs. — [Kosmo, 4 Jan 2024 (Thai case via Thaiger)](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/01/04/isteri-pantang-gosok-baju-suami/)
- (c) Corrected understanding: **This is GENUINELY DEBATED.**
  - Some classical jurists held that cooking, laundry and cleaning are not strictly the wife's duty. A speaker quoted in Utusan called that a weak opinion, while still urging husbands to help. — [Utusan, Dec 2018](https://www.utusan.com.my/nasional/2018/12/memasak-bukan-kewajiban-isteri/)
  - Muftiwp Irsyad al-Fatwa 304 cites al-Qaradawi that the wife's primary role is managing the home and family, while allowing careers under conditions. — [Muftiwp 304](https://www.muftiwp.gov.my/en/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum-cat/3391-irsyad-al-fatwa-siri-ke-304-hukum-wanita-berkerjaya)
  - Present this as a khilaf (scholarly difference) plus a matter of 'uruf and mutual agreement, not as a settled rule either way.

**Case 6 (reverse): Long-distance husband gives RM100/month nafkah** (Kosmo, 4 July 2025)
- (a) A woman on Threads complained that her husband, who works in another state and reportedly earns over RM3,000, gives only RM100 a month. His reason was that they don't live together, so he isn't obliged to give full nafkah. — [Kosmo, 4 July 2025](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2025/07/04/suami-pjj-beri-nafkah-rm100-sebulan-alasan-tak-tinggal-serumah/)
- (b) The misconception here is the *husband's*: "PJJ = nafkah boleh dikurangkan sesuka hati". The wife's complaint is broadly valid.
- (c) Correction:
  - The amount of nafkah is set by the husband's means (kaya, sederhana or fakir) according to local 'uruf (custom), as explained by the Pejabat Mufti WP. — [SKReact summarising Mufti WP](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/mufti-wilayah-jelas-penentuan-kadar-nafkah-ikut-kemampuan-suami)
  - Nafkah that has fallen due becomes the recipient's absolute property. — [USIM J. Fatwa paper](https://jfatwa.usim.edu.my/index.php/jfatwa/article/download/353/273/812)
  - Unpaid nafkah becomes a debt. — [Kosmo](https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=669665)
- Flip side (relevant to "nafkah = whole salary" misconception): Because nafkah is measured by kemampuan and kifayah (sufficiency), a wife is *not* entitled to the husband's whole salary. A wife demanding beyond his means is also addressed by the Mufti office. — [SKReact: hukum isteri menuntut di luar kemampuan suami](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/ketahui-hukum-isteri-menuntut-sesuatu-yang-diluar-kemampuan-suami-pejabat-mufti)

**Case 7 (reverse): "Dah macam housemate", husband splits all bills and gives no nafkah** (SAYS, date unclear)
- (a) A woman said her husband treats the marriage like a housemate arrangement: bills are split and no nafkah is given. Netizens replied that this is not a responsible husband. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/dah-macam-housemate-wanita-luah-rasa-kecewa-suami-terlalu-berkira-duit)
- (b) Misconception (husband's): "Isteri bekerja, jadi kongsi 50/50 menggantikan nafkah."
- (c) Correction: The husband must try his best to provide nafkah without relying on the wife's income (Dr. Zulkifli Mohamad al-Bakri, former Mufti WP). — [SKReact](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/suami-wajib-usaha-sebaik-mungkin-cari-nafkah-untuk-keluarga-tanpa-berharap-dengan-pendapatan-isteri-dr-zulkifli)
- Nuance: A wife may *voluntarily* contribute, and pooling income is a valid household arrangement. — [UiTM JCIS paper on income-sharing](https://jcis.uitm.edu.my/journal/volume2/issue1/9.%20Perkongsian%20Pendapatan%20Suami%20Isteri%20dalam%20Keluarga%20Islam%20dan%20Impak%20kepada%20Kualiti%20Hidup.pdf). The Utusan July 2026 rencana describes the trend of high-earning wives as "menteri kewangan" and experts naming nafkah and financial crises as top causes of breakdown. — [Utusan, July 2026](https://www.utusan.com.my/rencana/2026/07/apabila-isteri-jadi-menteri-kewangan-suami-suri-rumah/)

**Case 8: Woman earning RM7,000 vs husband RM4,000 complaining about funding the children** (X; reported via search summary; exact article URL uncertain)
- (a) Per a search summary: a woman on X whose salary rose to RM7,000 while her husband's stayed at RM4,000 complained about paying for the children. She was criticised as arrogant and ungrateful, though some defended her. **The summary did not tie this to a confirmed URL.** The closest SAYS headline in the results is a *man's* luahan about a higher-earning wife. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/awal-perkahwinan-dah-minta-macam-macam-lelaki-luah-beban-sebab-gaji-isteri-lebih-tinggi). Treat this case as **unverified; use only with caution.**
- (b) The misconception here is mainly the netizens': "gaji isteri tinggi = dia yang patut tanggung anak".
- (c) Correction: The husband's duty of nafkah stands even if the wife earns more. — [SKReact quoting Dr. Zulkifli](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/suami-wajib-usaha-sebaik-mungkin-cari-nafkah-untuk-keluarga-tanpa-berharap-dengan-pendapatan-isteri-dr-zulkifli)

**Case 9: "How do men earning below RM4k dare to marry?" Threads post on love vs income** (theSun and WeirdKaya; about a year old, so roughly late 2025, exact date unclear)
- (a) A Threads user asked how men earning below RM4K a month dare to marry, and asked wives in such households to share their experience. The user said it was a question for advice, not an insult. Netizen replies:
  - "it's not about money, it's about mindset";
  - one wife said her husband earned RM2,000 when they married and they made it work;
  - "the issue is lifestyle not matching income";
  - "RM4K really isn't enough at today's prices, but mutual support helps". — [theSun](https://thesun.my/going-viral/how-do-men-earning-below-rm4k-dare-to-marry-m-sian-s-post-sparks-heated-debate-on-love-vs-income-fi14584724/); [WeirdKaya](https://weirdkaya.com/msian-asks-how-men-earning-below-rm4k-dare-to-get-married-netizens-say-its-not-about-money-its-about-mindset/)
- (b) The poster's gender is **not confirmed** in the snippets. The case shows two opposing misconceptions: "cinta cukup, duit tak penting" versus "below a salary threshold you shouldn't marry".
- (c) Correction: Islamic family law ties nafkah to the husband's *kemampuan*, not to a fixed income threshold (see Case 6 sources). Financial problems are a leading cause of breakdown (Utusan, July 2026, above). Both extremes are oversimplified. This is a **debated** social question, not a settled one.

### Inferences
- The strongest "woman's own misconception" cases are 1–5. Cases 6–8 are better framed as "salah faham yang dibetulkan oleh luahan isteri", where the misconception sits with the husband or netizens.
- Recurring pattern: wives turn to Threads for "minta pendapat netizen". Netizens frequently push "cerai je" (red flag means leave), which is itself a misconception worth highlighting.

## What exactly is the mistaken belief, and how widespread is it (netizen reactions)?

### Takeaway
Netizen reactions are mostly sympathetic to wives on nafkah issues and hostile to husbands. They are split on poligami-facilitation, the housework division and the income-to-marry debate. A common netizen reflex is "cerai je", while the religious authorities consistently oppose public aib-exposure.

### Cited Findings
- Porn case: many netizens advised divorce. — [MustShareNews](https://mustsharenews.com/husband-loves-pornography/)
- Selekeh case: netizens were angry ("naik angin"), mostly at the husband. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/saya-selekeh-tak-pandai-layan-suami-punca-wanita-ini-bercerai-buat-netizen-naik-angin)
- Calon madu case: mixed reactions on Threads. — [Kosmo, 25 Jan 2026](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2026/01/25/isteri-cari-calon-bini-kedua-suami/)
- Housework and in-law case: netizens were split. Many called the mother-in-law's view outdated, while others said there is no right or wrong if the couple agree. — [Kosmo, 4 Jan 2024](https://www.kosmo.com.my/2024/01/04/isteri-pantang-gosok-baju-suami/)
- RM4k case: the majority said "mindset over money", with a minority pointing to cost of living. — [WeirdKaya](https://weirdkaya.com/msian-asks-how-men-earning-below-rm4k-dare-to-get-married-netizens-say-its-not-about-money-its-about-mindset/)
- Housemate case: netizens said such a man is not a responsible husband. — [SAYS](https://says.com/my/seismik/dah-macam-housemate-wanita-luah-rasa-kecewa-suami-terlalu-berkira-duit)

### Inferences
- The "aib" misconception is widespread enough that several state muftis (Perlis, Penang, Negeri Sembilan) have publicly addressed it.

## What do credible religious, legal or psychological sources say the correct understanding is, and is it settled or contested?

### Takeaway
The settled points are: nafkah follows the husband's means and stays his duty even if the wife works; poligami needs Syariah Court permission; public aib-exposure is condemned by muftis, with narrow exceptions. The contested points are: whether housework is the wife's duty (khilaf), whether to facilitate poligami, and the income-vs-love question. I found no Malaysian psychologist or LPPKN source in this round.

### Cited Findings
- **Nafkah follows the husband's kemampuan and 'uruf (settled).** — [SKReact/Mufti WP](https://skreact.siakapkeli.my/articles/mufti-wilayah-jelas-penentuan-kadar-nafkah-ikut-kemampuan-suami)
- **Nafkah that has fallen due is the wife's absolute property, and arrears are a debt (settled).** — [USIM J. Fatwa](https://jfatwa.usim.edu.my/index.php/jfatwa/article/download/353/273/812); [Kosmo](https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=669665)
- **Husbands can be nusyuz (settled within the Mufti WP framework).** — [Muftiwp 371](https://muftiwp.gov.my/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum/4005-irsyad-al-fatwa-siri-ke-371-nusyuz-bagi-seorang-suami)
- **Poligami requires Syariah Court permission, s.23 Act 303 (settled law).** — [UIS paper](https://conference.uis.edu.my/pasak5/images/eprosidingpasak5/ID045.pdf); [Act 303](https://lawplayer.com/my/act/act-303)
- **Exposing a spouse's aib publicly is haram per the Perlis Mufti. This is broad consensus among the muftis quoted, with exceptions for complaints of zulm or seeking legal help.** — [Kosmo](https://www.kosmo.com.my/?p=761173); [SAYS](https://says.com/my/gaya-hidup/hukum-dedah-konflik-rumah-tangga-di-media-sosial-tak-semua-perkara-layak-jadi-content)
- **Housework is CONTESTED (classical khilaf).** — [Utusan 2018](https://www.utusan.com.my/nasional/2018/12/memasak-bukan-kewajiban-isteri/); [Muftiwp 304](https://www.muftiwp.gov.my/en/artikel/irsyad-fatwa/irsyad-fatwa-umum-cat/3391-irsyad-al-fatwa-siri-ke-304-hukum-wanita-berkerjaya)

### Inferences
- In content for the target audience, the safest framing on housework is "khilaf; tentukan bersama secara ma'ruf". The nafkah and poligami-procedure points can be stated firmly.

### Gaps
- No LPPKN, Malaysian psychologist or counsellor quotes were found on porn-related self-esteem, self-blame after divorce, or "red flag means leave". This is a priority for follow-up.
- No source was found on whether a wife's condition such as "madu tak boleh ada anak" is enforceable.
- The text of s.59 Act 303 (nafkah and nusyuz) was not retrieved. Verify it via the AGC LOM portal.
- A direct muftiwp.gov.my ruling specifically on "isteri wajib buat kerja rumah?" was not located.
